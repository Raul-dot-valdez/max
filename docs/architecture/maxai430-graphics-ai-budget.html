maxAI 430: graphics and AI under worst-case load

Can a 4.3-inch maxAI 430-class display run TouchGFX graphics, a camera view and on-device AI at the same time on an STM32H743 with 2 MB of SDRAM? Six mower scenarios, the numbers behind them, an expert debate and the design rules that follow.

Verdict

The AI is not the bottleneck. On this hardware, the SDRAM bus and the shared external flash are. Get those two right and today's AI and graphics fit together with margin. Camera-based AI does not fit, and belongs on the next-generation MCU.

AI costThe phase 1 CNN takes 0.33–0.65 ms every 30 s, which is 0.002 % of the CPU. The LSTM candidate takes 3.8 ms at most. Running at a lower priority than the GUI, either fits in the gaps between frames.
Real limitWith 24-bit frame buffers, the worst-of-worst scenario uses 84 % of the SDRAM bandwidth available. The risk is the display controller running short of pixels, which shows up as flicker. With 16-bit (RGB565) frame buffers it drops to 59 %. The 24-bit panel still gets a dithered image.
Hidden riskErasing a sector of the external flash blocks reads for up to 400 ms. If the alarm screens load their images from the same chip, an alarm can freeze on screen just when the engine faults.
Out of reachPerson or obstacle detection on the camera needs 225–375 ms per frame, which gives about 2–3.5 fps. That is too slow to be useful, and it is not a safety function. This workload belongs on the STM32N6 and its NPU.
ST pathGenerate the models with ST Edge AI Core. Benchmark them on real H7 silicon in ST Edge AI Developer Cloud before the 430 hardware arrives. Build TouchGFX and X-CUBE-AI in one STM32CubeMX project, and use NanoEdge AI for the per-machine blade-wear trend.

Detail

Each scenario, its load and its fix

One frame

S2 inside one 16.7 ms frame

Full-screen slide at 60 fps while the CNN fires. The DMA2D copy has to share the SDRAM with the display controller's scan-out. Its time is the bytes it moves divided by the bandwidth left over: 783 KB / 86.5 MB/s = 9.1 ms with 24-bit buffers, and 522 KB / 94.3 MB/s = 5.5 ms with 16-bit buffers. The AI runs in whatever CPU time is free.

CPU work for the GUI is 1.5 ms of framework plus 1.3 ms of widgets per frame. CAN interrupts are 3 µs each, about 54 per frame at 40 % load on two buses. The AI task has the lowest priority above idle, so a frame is never late because of the AI.

Debate

Six views around the table

ST edge-AI application engineerStay inside the ST flow. Run ST Edge AI Core analyze and validate on both phase 1 models today. Get real Cortex-M7 cycle counts from Developer Cloud this week. Put X-CUBE-AI next to TouchGFX in one CubeMX project, so one linker script owns the memory map.
Graphics lead (TouchGFX)Half a millisecond of AI every 30 seconds is nothing to me. 24-bit frame buffers on a 16-bit SDRAM are. I want RGB565 with LTDC dithering, transitions capped while the camera is on, and the needle images kept small for the texture mapper.
Memory and bus architectBudget against 110 MB/s, never the 200 MB/s on the datasheet. The display controller is the one master that cannot wait, so give it priority on the bus. Keep the AI out of SDRAM and QSPI: activations in DTCM, weights in internal flash.
Mower system architectOn a zero-turn, the operator needs the alarm readable at once and the camera clear when reversing. Slide animations are nice to have. Dealers will ask for person detection. On this display it can only be advisory, and at 2–3 fps it is not worth shipping.
Functional safety and CRA leadThe AI stays advisory, with no machine control. Telltales and alarms must never depend on the external flash or on the AI task. Under CRA Annex I, model updates are firmware: signed, versioned and covered by secure boot, and each AI alert is logged.
Sourcing and portability skepticST's tools are strong, but they ship a closed runtime library. Keep the ai_backend_t interface, keep a TFLM reference build in CI and keep the GD32 path alive. Going with ST should be a choice we keep making, not one we can't undo.
Resolution. ST Edge AI Core is the production path on the H743. TFLM stays as the open reference build, and the two must agree on the test set. The graphics rules below are mandatory. Camera AI moves to an STM32N6 generation. Nobody disagreed that the AI cost is negligible today. The argument was about bandwidth and flash.
Rules

Design rules that follow

  1. R1Use RGB565 double frame buffers in SDRAM, with LTDC dithering on. This saves 255 KB and a third of the scan-out traffic.
  2. R2Give the LTDC priority on the AXI bus. Count FIFO underrun interrupts and log them as a field health metric.
  3. R3Run AI in a dedicated task at the lowest priority above idle. Put activations in DTCM and weights in internal flash, never in QSPI flash or SDRAM.
  4. R4Keep alarm and telltale assets in internal flash or cache them in SDRAM at boot. Never let them depend on QSPI being readable.
  5. R5The data log uses W25Q erase-suspend, or pre-erases sectors while idle, or writes to internal flash bank 2. No erase starts while an alarm is being drawn.
  6. R6No full-screen 60 fps fades or slides while the camera view is shown. Use instant switches or 30 fps covers.
  7. R7Decimate the camera 2:1 in the DCMI and convert colour in a task below the GUI. Check first whether the 430i camera path has a hardware converter.
  8. R8Decode DM1 multi-packet messages at task level. The CAN interrupt only queues frames.
  9. R9Plan every budget at 400 MHz. Treat 480 MHz as headroom, not capacity.
  10. R10All AI output is advisory and logged as EV_AI_HEALTH. No camera AI on the H743 generation.
